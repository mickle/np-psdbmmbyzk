How the model works

Seller note. The land is bought with a seller note (0% down by default) whose simple interest accrues and is paid with the principal at the sale closing. The note is subordinated to the construction loan. The model flags a timeline longer than the note term.

Construction loan. The loan funds the loan-to-cost share of the non-land project budget (design, permits, utilities, building, site, pool, the full contingency budget and the GC qualifier fee). Interest is paid monthly on the drawn balance: the loan draws on a monthly schedule over the build (front-loaded, S-curve or back-loaded), then stays fully drawn through the sale period. Points and note stamps/intangible tax are charged on the loan amount.

Owner cash. The owner funds the rest of the project budget, any land down payment, land and loan closing, construction interest, property tax and insurance. Only the contingency actually spent counts as a cost; the unspent remainder comes back at the sale.

GC qualifier. A licensed general contractor serves as the license-holder/qualifier while the owner's team manages the build; general conditions and site supervision are already inside the hard $/sf. Florida qualifier fees are typically a monthly retainer (≈$500–$5,000+) or ≈1–3% of revenue. Under Fla. Stat. §489.1195 the qualifier must actually supervise the work and the finances, and the arrangement has to be bona fide.

Sale. Sale costs are the buyer-side commission, documentary stamps on the deed and the promulgated owner's title premium. Break-even and 15%-margin sale $/sf are solved against those costs.
